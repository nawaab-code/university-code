## Syllabus

### Unit I — Photonics

- **Lasers.** Laser characteristics, radiation–matter interaction, derivation and significance of the energy-density equation, and conditions for laser action. Principles, construction, and operation of helium–neon and semiconductor lasers. Holography, lidar, and numerical problems.
- **Optical fibers.** Total internal reflection, propagation, acceptance angle, numerical aperture, fractional index change, propagation modes, mode count, V parameter, fiber types, attenuation, and the expression for the attenuation coefficient. Point-to-point optical communication with a block diagram, advantages and limitations, and numerical problems.

### Unit II — Quantum Mechanics

- **Matter waves.** Wave–particle duality, the de Broglie hypothesis, derivation of the wavelength by analogy, and derivation of the relation between phase and group velocity. The Heisenberg uncertainty principle and its relativistic application to electron confinement in the nucleus.
- **Wave mechanics.** Wave functions and the Born interpretation, the time-independent Schrödinger equation, eigenfunctions and eigenvalues, and wave functions and probabilities for a particle in a one-dimensional infinite potential well. Numerical problems.

### Unit III — Electrical Properties of Metals and Semiconductors

- **Metals.** Resistivity, mobility, phonons, Matthiessen’s rule, quantum free-electron theory, and Fermi–Dirac statistics. Fermi energy, the dependence of the Fermi factor on temperature and energy, and derivations of the density of states and Fermi energy.
- **Semiconductors.** Derive the conduction-band electron concentration and state the valence-band hole concentration. Cover the law of mass action, conductivity, and the Hall effect, including derivation and applications of the Hall coefficient. Numerical problems.

### Unit IV — Superconductivity and Dielectrics

- **Superconductivity.** Temperature dependence of resistivity and critical field, the Meissner effect, critical current, types of superconductors, quantum tunneling, and high-temperature superconductivity. Qualitative treatment of BCS theory, Josephson junctions, and DC and RF SQUIDs. Charge, phase, and flux qubits.
- **Dielectrics.** Polar and nonpolar dielectrics, polarization mechanisms, internal fields in solids, and derivation of the Clausius–Mossotti equation. Dielectric losses, the piezoelectric effect, ferroelectrics, and numerical problems.

### Unit V — Quantum Computing

- **Experiments and foundations.** Construction and operation of the Stern–Gerlach experiment. Production and detection of plane, circular, and elliptical polarization. Moore’s law, single-particle interference, classical and quantum information and computing, and quantum superposition.
- **Qubits and operators.** Qubit states, probability normalization, the Bloch sphere, ket and matrix representations, and the action of identity and Pauli operators on basis states. Inner products, probabilities, orthogonality, and orthonormality.
- **Quantum gates.** Quantum NOT, Pauli Z, Hadamard, S (phase), and T gates. Controlled gates, CNOT, and model realizations.

## Course outcomes

| Outcome | On completion, students can |
| --- | --- |
| CO1 | Explain the principles and applications of photonic devices. |
| CO2 | Distinguish phase and group velocity and solve the time-independent Schrödinger equation for an infinite potential well. |
| CO3 | Apply quantum theory to electrical conductivity in metals and to superconductivity. |
| CO4 | Describe the Hall effect and applications of semiconductor devices. |
| CO5 | Illustrate applications of quantum computing to design and data analysis. |

All five course outcomes map to PO1, PO2, and PO12.
